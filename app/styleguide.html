Principles

Who we design for: someone on a shared, cracked Android phone, outdoors, reading slowly, maybe in their second language, maybe with no signal. Every rule below comes from them.

Thumbs first

Anything a thumb aims at is at least 56 px. The four main places sit at the bottom.

Icon plus word

Every action shows both. The icon helps a reader who cannot read the word, the word helps one who cannot read the icon.

Play, Save, Share

Three verbs carry the whole app. Plain words, the same words on every screen.

Readable in sunlight

17 px body text, and every text colour at least 4.5:1 on its ground, in light and dark.

Every script

Arabic joins, Ethiopic and Devanagari get their own faces and line height, right-to-left mirrors.

Calm, kind motion

Short, never in the way, transform and opacity only, and none at all when the phone asks for less.

Colour

Purple for the brand, gold for light, green for Play and success, sky for information. Components never use a colour directly: they ask for its job (text, fill-go, wash-info), and light and dark answer differently.

Brand palette

Text and fills, checked live

Each pair shows its contrast ratio, worked out in your browser from the current theme. Switch the theme at the top to check dark.

Type

System faces only, so nothing downloads. On iPhone and Mac the rounded face (SF Pro Rounded) gives the friendly, chunky look; everywhere else falls back to the phone's own sans.

Motion

Durations are named by their job. Four curves cover everything. Under "reduce motion" every duration collapses to nothing, so the app still works and simply stops moving. Try it with the Motion switch at the top.

Icons

One 24 px grid, 2 px round strokes, drawn in the text colour. Always beside a word, never alone, except on a round icon button that has a spoken label.

Buttons et-btn

A chunky face on a darker lip, so it looks like something to press even to someone new to touchscreens. Press one: the face drops onto the lip.

QR sharing et-qr · et-scanner

Phone to phone, any brand: one phone shows a code, the other points its camera. The code is always black on white whatever the theme, because cameras misread inverted codes.

Kandili, the lantern ET.mascot

Swahili for "lamp". Flat art cut into parts (body, flame, eyes, mouth, arms) and moved by code, the way Duolingo's owl works, with nothing to download. The flame is the emotion meter: tall when happy, low when waiting. These shapes follow the concept sheet; when the final ChatGPT art is traced, only the drawing changes.

Moods: the loop underneath

Ten poses, one per moment in the app

Using it

<script src="assets/js/brand/mascot.js"></script>

var k = ET.mascot.create(el, { pose: 'hello', size: '8rem' });
k.moment('saved');                 // pose + move + mood, in one call
k.play('hop').then(next);          // one move; resolves when it ends
ET.mascot.markup('share', { size: 48 });   // a still SVG string

<div data-kandili data-moment="welcome"></div>   // no script needed per page
